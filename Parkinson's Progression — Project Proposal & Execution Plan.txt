Part 1 — Refined Project Proposal
1. Objective
Model individual-level longitudinal progression of Parkinson's disease by fusing
multimodal clinical, biomarker, and imaging-derived data from the PPMI cohort, using
deep learning architectures suited to irregular longitudinal data, with rigorous leakage-
safe evaluation. Extend to smartphone-derived (mPower) signals as a secondary
objective if data access, quality, and time allow.
2. Research Questions
RQ1 (core): Can [MDS-UPDRS Part III motor score] at 12 and 24 months be
predicted more accurately from a patient's full longitudinal multimodal visit history than
from a single baseline visit alone?
RQ2 (core): Among early (feature-level), late (decision-level), and attention-based
(learned) fusion of clinical, biomarker, and imaging-derived modalities, which strategy
gives the best — and most stable across prediction horizons — performance?
RQ3 (optional extension): Does adding mPower smartphone-derived voice, tapping,
and gait features as an auxiliary modality improve on the PPMI-only model, despite
device heterogeneity and crowdsourced noise?
Week 1 team decision: confirm or replace the bracketed target in RQ1. MDS-
UPDRS Part III (motor score) is recommended — it's the most consistently collected
scale across PPMI visits and maps directly to clinically meaningful severity.
Whatever you choose, it must be this specific, not "progression" in general.
3. Prediction Task Definition
Unit of prediction: one patient, at one point in their visit history
Input: all visits up to and including time t

=== PAGE 2 ===
Output: target score at t + 12 months and t + 24 months (two horizons, evaluated
separately)
Task type: regression (primary); optional secondary framing as binary
classification ("meaningful worsening" vs. "stable," using an established clinical
threshold) for interpretability
Hard rule: no visit after time t may contribute to any feature used to predict at time
t. This is checked explicitly during data engineering, never assumed.
4. Data & Cohort
Primary: PPMI — clinical/motor/non-motor assessments, biospecimen markers,
imaging-derived measures (DAT-SPECT striatal binding ratios, structural MRI
regional volumes)
Cohort: de novo PD, prodromal, and healthy control arms — finalize exact
inclusion criteria in Week 1 once data access is confirmed
Secondary (stretch): mPower — voice, finger-tapping, gait, and tremor from
smartphone sensors (~58,000 recordings, ~5,800 participants)
MVP scope: imaging as derived features, not raw 3D volumes. Raw-volume 3D
CNN work is a stretch goal only (Part 2, Phase 4).
5. Methodology
5.1 Pipeline overview
flowchart TD
    A[Raw PPMI tables: clinical, biomarker, imaging-derived] --> B[Spark E
    B --> C[Patient-level train/val/test split]
    C --> D[Clinical and biomarker encoder: GRU-D or time-aware Transforme
    C --> E[Imaging-derived encoder: FFN or CNN]
    D --> F[Fusion layer: early, late, attention - compared head to head]
    E --> F
    F --> G[Prediction: target at +12 and +24 months]
    G --> H[Explainability: SHAP, attention, Grad-CAM]
5.2 Big Data component — where Spark is actually load-bearing
PPMI alone (~1,000 multimodal patients) is not large by volume — don't pretend
otherwise in the report. Spark earns its place in two specific jobs:
1. Longitudinal feature engineering across the full cohort's visit history:
windowed/rolling statistics, time-since-baseline features, missing-visit masks — a
genuine distributed job over all patients × all visits × all modalities.
2. (If RQ3 is pursued) mPower raw-signal processing: feature extraction from tens
of thousands of raw voice/accelerometer recordings — genuinely high-volume, and
the strongest justification for distributed processing in this project.

=== PAGE 3 ===
5.3 Train/val/test protocol
Split by patient ID, never by visit/row. Compute the split once, save it as a shared
table ( patient_id → split ), and have every team member load that same file.
Nobody recomputes their own split.
5.4 Baselines (built before any fusion work)
Per-modality classical ML on the most recent visit only (cross-sectional baseline)
Per-modality simple sequence model (plain LSTM) on the full visit history
These exist to prove the pipeline works end-to-end and to give a real yardstick for the
fusion models later.
5.5 Modality encoders
Clinical/biomarker time series → GRU-D or a time-embedded Transformer (built to
handle irregular visit spacing directly, not assume uniform time steps)
Imaging-derived features → feedforward network or small CNN
Stretch: raw 3D imaging CNN, following Dentamaro et al.'s architecture — only
after the above is solid
5.6 Fusion strategies (compared head-to-head)
Early (feature-level concatenation), late (decision-level ensembling), and attention-
based (learned) fusion — all three implemented and benchmarked, not just one
chosen by assumption.
5.7 Explainability
SHAP for tabular/derived-feature contributions; attention-weight visualization for the
sequence/fusion model; Grad-CAM if a raw-imaging CNN is built.
5.8 Stability evaluation
Re-run the fusion comparison across both prediction horizons (12 vs. 24 months). If
the winning fusion strategy changes between horizons, that instability is itself a real,
reportable finding for RQ2 — not a bug to hide.
6. Evaluation Metrics
Regression: MAE, RMSE, R²
Secondary classification framing: F1, AUROC, calibration
Comparing fusion strategies: paired significance testing (e.g., bootstrap), not raw
metric differences alone

Transformers).

imaging-derived variables PPMI provides pre-computed vs. what would need raw
processing.

Phase 2 — Data Engineering (Weeks 2–4)
Goal: Clean, joined, leakage-safe modeling tables.
Data Engineering Lead (owns this phase): Build the Spark pipeline — ingest raw
PPMI exports, join clinical/biomarker/imaging-derived tables by patient + visit,
engineer time-respecting rolling/windowed features, handle missing visits (masks,
time-delta features for GRU-D), create the canonical patient-level split and save it
as the shared artifact.
Time-Series Lead: Define the clinical/biomarker feature list with the Data Lead;
build the time-series data loader; run EDA on visit frequency, missingness patterns,
and target-score distributions.
Imaging/Fusion Lead: Extract the specific imaging-derived variables to use; if raw
imaging is a later stretch goal, research the preprocessing tooling now (don't
discover this in week 10).
Joint: Review the split and EDA together; freeze the feature list per modality before
modeling starts.
Deliverable: Clean modeling tables per modality + saved patient-level split + EDA
summary.
Phase 3 — Baselines (Weeks 4–6)
Goal: Simple, working, single-modality baselines — the pipeline proven end-to-end,
and a real yardstick for later.
Data Engineering Lead: Build the shared evaluation harness (given predictions +
targets, computes MAE/RMSE/R² and produces the comparison table everyone
uses downstream).
Time-Series Lead: Train a cross-sectional baseline (XGBoost/LightGBM on the
latest visit only) and a longitudinal baseline (plain LSTM on the full
clinical/biomarker history).

Phase 4 — Modality-Specific Deep Encoders (Weeks 6–9)
Goal: A real deep encoder per modality, each beating its own baseline.
Time-Series Lead (owns): Implement GRU-D or a time-embedded Transformer;
tune against the Phase 3 LSTM baseline.
Imaging/Fusion Lead (owns): Implement the imaging-derived feature encoder;
only after this is solid, prototype a raw-imaging 3D CNN as a stretch add-on.
Data Engineering Lead: Support both with pipeline adjustments as needed; if
RQ3 is still in scope, start the mPower raw-signal Spark pipeline now, since it's the
most time-consuming Big Data piece.
Joint: Short weekly sync to catch integration issues (ID format mismatches,
inconsistent scaling) before fusion time.
Deliverable: Two validated modality encoders, each beating its Phase 3 baseline
(or a documented reason why not).
Phase 5 — Fusion & Comparison (Weeks 9–11)
Goal: Answer RQ2 rigorously.
Imaging/Fusion Lead (owns): Implement early, late, and attention-based fusion
combining both encoders.
Time-Series Lead: Support the clinical/biomarker side of fusion; help run the multi-
horizon (12 vs. 24 month) evaluation.
Data Engineering Lead: Run paired significance testing between fusion strategies
and baselines using the shared harness.
Joint: Interpret results honestly — a marginal fusion gain over the best single
modality is a legitimate, expected finding, not a failure.
Deliverable: Full fusion comparison table + significance tests — RQ2 answered.
Phase 6 — Explainability & Stability (Weeks 11–12)
Goal: Interpret the winning model; verify the stability claim in RQ2.
Imaging/Fusion Lead: SHAP for feature contributions; Grad-CAM if a raw-imaging
CNN was built.
